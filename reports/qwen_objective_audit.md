# Qwen training objective audit

**EXP-027 already trained Qwen LoRA with a differentiable within-painting Pearson objective and measured a nonzero gradient from that term alone.** The loss was BCE plus stabilized Pearson on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. The exact unsmoothed evaluator was used for reporting. I found no run that optimized unregularized scored Pearson end to end, and no such run on the current Qwen3-VL-4B architecture. The direct-metric mechanism itself has been tested. Its measured result and the current Qwen3 incumbent gap do not justify another similar multi-hour pilot.

On one six-language fitting painting, the original L4 run measured **0.001734** LoRA gradient norm from the high arm's weighted Pearson term alone versus **0.060077** from BCE (**2.89%**). The code computes the complete-window logit derivative then replays exact same-chunk logits and backpropagates that derivative into the actual head and decoder adapters. It fails on unequal replay logits. Both treatments recorded nonzero training gradient norms and selected adapter update norms (1.0173 and 1.0086). The one-panel gradient ratio does not quantify contribution throughout training. Optimization did respond to Pearson. Training Pearson loss fell over two epochs, yet neither selected model improved validation Contrast. All three validation BCE curves worsened at epoch 2 and epoch-2 Contrast was not retained. The scores are conditional on the small 192/48 split and the BCE-selected epoch. ## Metric and CPU verification

The organizer specification and public starter implementation agree on unweighted Pearson between predicted probabilities and observed positive shares across each painting's supplied languages, followed by an equal mean across paintings. Each supplied pair needs at least five eligible votes. A painting needs three or more such languages and nonconstant gold; a constant prediction on an eligible painting contributes zero. Full Development uses the released keys, including English, Arabic and Chinese. Official hidden ST3 excludes those languages and covers 21 languages. Test-like P2 drops those three and recalculates eligibility. The 20-pair minimum applies to the supplementary per-language Macro Log Loss, not to which supplied rows enter Contrast. The public starter scorer was inspected and replayed. The hosted executable was unavailable behind the Codabench bot challenge, so hosted binary parity is unverified. [Organizer specification](https://github.com/artemotion-28/artemotion-28/blob/516d7641c3c1d099e63aa8d066971c6b07c89384/README.md#evaluation) · [Starter scorer](https://github.com/artemotion-28/artemotion-28/blob/516d7641c3c1d099e63aa8d066971c6b07c89384/starter_kit/starter_kit.ipynb) · [Local scorer](../src/st3/metrics.py#L13)

For centered probabilities `q_c` and centered observed shares `y_c`, EXP-027 uses
`r_eps = dot(q_c, y_c / norm(y_c)) / sqrt(norm(q_c)^2 + eps^2)`
and averages `1 - r_eps` within each optimizer window. Missing rows are absent, low-count rows are filtered and flat gold is skipped. The denominator attenuates scores for low-spread predictions and gives a finite useful gradient at constant predictions. Exact scored Pearson is discontinuous there and has no gradient matching that scoring convention. The smoothing also changes the loss on nonconstant groups. It does not remove vote sampling noise; vote-weighted BCE provides a probabilistic anchor, while the Pearson term trusts each eligible painting equally within its window. Window normalization and optimizer updates also differ from maximizing one single macro mean on all training predictions simultaneously.

CPU verification matched 15 run-pinned EXP-027 source and lock hashes, the three selected tensors and CSVs, and all recorded EXP-030/031 epoch score receipts made available. The public starter function and the local scorer agreed on uneven and missing panels, a low-vote row, flat gold and constant predictions. The small-epsilon limit reproduced **0.341578306566** within `1e-12`; the historical .01 smoothing yielded **0.341409309074** on that fixture. Numerical gradient checking passed at constant and near-constant predictions. A Pearson-only split-versus-unsplit synthetic check gave the same adapter gradients within FP32 tolerance, with frozen base gradients absent. The exact EXP-027 CPU checkout passed **28 tests**. CUDA component gradients are archived measurements, not freshly rerun. Local replay script and log are under `runs/qwen_objective_audit/`.

## Competition decision

**Keep Corrected Ridge and do not repeat the Pearson-loss experiment on Qwen at this point.** EXP-027 answers whether complete-group backpropagation can reach real Qwen adapters. Its two mild objective weights do not rule out stronger Pearson or a better 4B readout. There is no matched evidence that either would bridge the 4B run's **−0.096974** test-like fold-3 deficit or its **+0.096986 bits** Log Loss gap. EXP-031's ranking loss and EXP-030's metric-based checkpoint selection cannot fill that evidence gap. Frequent clipping in prior runs is an observation without a matched intervention that establishes causality.

The recent RTX 4060 run measured **3.76 / 3.95 GiB allocated/reserved** for the adapted arm and **2,875 s training + 643 s held-out inference per epoch** on average. A full two-epoch comparison with *two* adapted arms would take roughly **3.9 hours** at those measured rates before any extra Pearson replay. Adding a forward-only replay to the treatment, scaled from the observed held-out decode rate, puts it around **5.3 hours** before setup and reserve. Actual replay throughput and memory remain unmeasured. Since direct painting-level Pearson was already tested, this audit adds no new pilot or GPU authorization request. The production CSV and Corrected Ridge ZIP retain their recorded hashes.
