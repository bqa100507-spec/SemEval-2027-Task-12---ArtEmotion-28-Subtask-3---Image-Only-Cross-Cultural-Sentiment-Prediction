# EXP-038 recommendation — shared visual factors for cultural deviations

> **SUPERSEDED (2026-11-26).** EXP-003 showed rank-2/4/8/16 interactions losing to the full interaction on CLIP-B (0.394/0.406/0.387/0.367 vs 0.436), so a rank-8 shared basis risks repeating that failure. The approved EXP-038 is the [cultural-deviation ensemble](../docs/exp038_kaggle.md). This file is kept as the historical recommendation.

**Recommended for owner review.** Use a VLM-free SigLIP2 visual branch with explicit global sentiment, training-only language priors and a small shared basis for image-dependent cultural deviations. A matched control should keep the same visual training and fit a regularized full interaction readout. The question is whether sharing response factors improves within-painting ordering and provides residual diversity beyond Ridge and EXP-036.

The proposed representation lets several shared visual factors be combined differently by language and separates the static prior by design. EXP-032 shared statistical strength through a learned graph on frozen slopes. EXP-031 used a global Qwen vector, rank-32 readout and ranking simultaneously. Emotion counts provide additional within-polarity evidence and allow exact count likelihood. Changing from an independent auxiliary classifier to two conditional four-emotion distributions tied to the binary logit makes the emotional and binary heads consistent. ### One proposed experiment

Use EXP-036's pinned SigLIP2 So400m 384 processor and fresh rank-32 adapters on the last six blocks. Combine pooled native features with four shared learned patch queries in a compact, normalized pooling head. The queries consume pixels only and run once per artwork. Both arms train the same encoder modules and pooler on the entire official Train population. More visual training is justified if it improves cultural ordering under this control.

Let (h_i) denote the visual vector, (g(h_i)) a global sentiment term, and (b_l) a fitting-only Beta(1,1) positive-rate logit, centered over the supported vocabulary. Define

```text
f_i = U (h_i - h_fit)
d_il = (e_l - sum_j w_j e_j)^T f_i
logit(p_il) = g(h_i) + b_l + d_il
```

Use eight factors in (f_i). The fixed weights (w_j) normalize training language pair support. Estimate the visual anchor (h_	ext{fit}) once as the Train-only mean representation at the common pretrained initialization, then freeze it with the bundle. This makes the prior the intercept at that reference point. A changing encoder can still shift the learned feature mean away from the anchor. An unsupported official language uses the shared score and the mean prior, with zero deviation. L2 regularize (U) and (e_l), with stronger shrinkage for small-support language loadings and a single preregistered strength rule. The matched arm uses (d_{il}) from a regularized full linear readout on the same visual vector, vocabulary centering and fitting-only anchor. Both systems fit directly to official votes and use no language metadata or other languages’ answers.

Keep vote-weighted binomial BCE primary. Hold eight-emotion CE and the verified Train-only caption target mechanism fixed across both arms if reused. Compare the architecture with the existing supervision instead of adding a new Pearson or ranking term. EXP-004’s best Pearson gain was +0.001834 under its original setup and fell short of promotion. EXP-027 verified Pearson gradients yet trailed BCE. EXP-031 combined a confidence-weighted ranking loss with other changes and underperformed. EXP-035's directly supervised Ridge residuals changed predictions by very little and did not help fold 3. The EXP-037 oracle shows why plain mean correction and positive rescaling cannot solve the ordering problem. A centered continuous objective or pairwise term would need a separately controlled, noise-aware comparison after the readout establishes a meaningful deficit.

Predeclare a matched six-epoch schedule and single optimizer recipe before Development scoring. Verify separate gradient reach for all active losses on several positive-mass Train paintings. Save complete epoch checkpoints with component losses, rank agreement, centered RMSE and dispersion. Select by full Development Contrast, Log Loss, then earliest epoch. Show both arms standalone alongside Corrected Ridge and EXP-036. Evaluate a single fixed 20% blend with EXP-036 alongside the standalone comparisons. Run the established Evaluation-like filter, paired painting uncertainty, and sensitivity to trimming high-impact wins. Development remains a selection panel. This proposal adds no CV and uses no hidden Evaluation labels.

**Falsifiable result.** If the rank-8 interaction does not improve ordering over the matched full readout, or neither its standalone nor its fixed incumbent blend improves Evaluation-like Contrast while protecting full Contrast and Log Loss, the shared-basis justification fails. A practically useful result would be around **+0.005** Evaluation-like Contrast over the frozen incumbent with positive full Contrast, loss within **+0.005 bits**, and improvement surviving removal of the largest 5% of absolute painting deltas. Report smaller or conflicting results without changing the rule. These limits inform the next architecture decision and do not govern archive generation.

EXP-036 measured about three fitting minutes per epoch on a T4. Shared token pooling adds activation and attention cost to that lower bound. Reserve **about two T4 GPU-hours** for both six-epoch arms, real-input preflight, verification and export. This is a planning estimate. Confirm actual throughput and memory with the pinned recipe before the experiment. The candidate uses one SigLIP2 pass per painting and four shared regions, with no decoder or required extra checkpoint families. Eligibility review still applies to the complete training recipe.
